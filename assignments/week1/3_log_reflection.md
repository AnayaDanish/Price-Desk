# AI Log and Reflection

## Part 1: AI Challenge

My original questions were:

1. How do the prices of everyday food products vary across the recorded dates?
2. Which everyday food products have the highest and lowest recorded prices?

AI suggested making these questions more specific and focused on comparisons that could be answered using the available data.

The improved questions are:

1. How did the average recorded price of each product change across the 12 recorded dates?
2. Which product had the highest and lowest average recorded price across the dataset?

These questions are more precise because they explain what is being compared and make it easier to analyse the data.

## Part 2: Checking AI Suggestions

The dataset contains 96 observations, 4 different products, 12 dates and 2 cities. It includes the columns date, item, city, price, unit and data_label.

The following AI suggestions could be explored using the available columns:

- Comparing the average prices of the four products.
- Examining how recorded prices changed across the dates.
- Comparing the recorded prices of products between the two cities.

However, not every suggested question can be answered using this dataset.

1. Can we determine whether these products are affordable for an average household? No, because the dataset does not contain household income or expenditure information.

2. Can we explain why the prices of these products increased or decreased? No, because there is no information about factors such as supply, demand, taxes or production costs.

There was no separate data dictionary provided with the files, so I checked the available CSV columns and sample records instead. The data_label column identifies the dataset as illustrative teaching data, not actual PBS observations.

## Part 3: Reflection

This exercise helped me understand that having data does not mean every question can be answered with it. The dataset allowed me to compare the recorded prices of different products, examine changes across dates and identify differences between two cities. However, it could not explain why prices changed or whether products were affordable for households. I also learned that it is important to check what information a dataset actually contains before accepting an AI-generated suggestion. Since this is illustrative teaching data and not actual PBS data, its findings should not be treated as evidence of real market price trends.
